# Compliance Notes

NiveshNadi Phase 1 must remain a self-research facility.

## Allowed in Phase 1

- Educational fund category explanations.
- Objective fund comparison.
- SIP, STP, SWP, and goal calculators.
- Risk stress tests that show hypothetical drawdown impact, recovery assumptions, and behavior prompts.
- Bad market rehearsals that turn hypothetical drawdowns into no-panic rules, evidence checks, and copyable pre-stress action lanes without telling the investor what transaction to execute.
- Market panic triage prompts that separate allowed research actions from locked transaction-like actions during drawdown pressure without giving a buy, sell, switch, redeem, hold, or SIP instruction.
- Cost reality checks that use investor-entered assumptions for TER drag, exit load, and tax friction without calculating tax liability.
- Investor readiness gates that check whether research discipline is complete before a user writes a memo or reviews an action.
- Portfolio overlap and concentration analysis.
- User-written decision journal.
- General research prompts and risk explanations.
- Category Playbook routes that explain category-level research lanes before fund shortlisting.
- Suitability Passport prompts that compare a local-only research profile with fund signals without claiming personal suitability.
- Goal-fund fit heatmaps that map general goal lenses to fund roles without claiming personal suitability.
- Red flag prompts that highlight research risks, missing evidence, overlap, concentration, and review needs without rejecting or approving a fund.
- Switch Decision Lab prompts that convert fund-change concerns into research lanes without recommending a transaction.
- Peer benchmark boards that compare objective category and sleeve signals without making a personalized best-fund claim.
- Category-level goal mapping that avoids naming a personalized best fund.
- Guided investor routes that connect research tools while staying category-led.
- Side-by-side fund comparisons that describe role, cost, risk, evidence readiness, and research questions.
- Retail subscription pricing for research workflow features such as saved history, exports, watchlists, and alerts.
- Watchlist triggers that prompt review without saying buy, sell, switch, or hold.
- Decision packs that summarize user-selected evidence, stated intent, risks, watchlist triggers, and next review discipline.
- Portfolio X-Ray summaries that describe category concentration, role clarity, duplication risk, and research warnings without prescribing transactions.
- Portfolio blueprint drafts that let users test weights, sleeve mix, TER, evidence, and guardrails without saying the draft is suitable or recommended.
- Rebalance guard reviews that compare target and current weights, show drift bands, and route future contribution research without telling the user to trade.
- Portfolio review rooms that organize drift, evidence, cost, overlap, behavior, watchlist triggers, and next-review discipline without prescribing action.
- Review vault snapshots that compare review score, evidence, drift, queue size, and attention triggers over time without saying the portfolio is approved.
- Investor review records that package compact review metadata, decision boundaries, and source-check guardrails without storing regulated identifiers or giving action instructions.
- Research dossiers that assemble selected fund, compare set, evidence, review queue, investor record context, and source-check boundaries without claiming advice or suitability.
- Evidence source maps that show which research signals are demo, mapped, planned, or live-cited.
- Fund House Lens stewardship prompts that review process, people, cost, AUM/capacity, style stability, and evidence gaps without approving or rejecting a fund.
- Document decoding prompts that explain SID, KIM, factsheet, portfolio disclosure, riskometer, and TER clauses in plain research language without producing advice.
- Review rhythm prompts that schedule evidence checks, cost audits, behavior reviews, and portfolio reviews without creating transaction deadlines.

## Avoid until reviewed

- Personalized buy, sell, switch, hold, or redeem recommendations.
- Model portfolios mapped to a specific investor profile.
- Claims of suitability for a named investor.
- Suitability Passport language that says the app has certified, approved, or recommended a fund for a specific user.
- "Best fund for you" language inside the Goal Fit Compass.
- Category Playbook language that crowns a category or fund as personally suitable for a named investor.
- Goal-Fund Fit Heatmap language that says a fund is suitable for a named investor or that a user should allocate to it.
- Red Flag Radar language that says a user must sell, avoid, switch, or buy because a flag is present or absent.
- Switch Decision Lab language that says the user should switch, sell, redeem, hold, pause SIP, or execute a replacement fund.
- Peer Benchmark Board language that says the top-ranked peer is the right fund for a user.
- "You should buy this fund" language inside First SIP Coach.
- Compare Matrix language that crowns one fund as the personalized right choice.
- Stress Lab language that predicts a future fall, recovery, or investor action.
- Bad Market Rehearsal language that says the user should buy, sell, switch, hold, pause, continue, redeem, or increase because a stress score changed.
- Market Panic Triage language that treats the wait window, proof route, or triage score as permission or instruction to execute a transaction.
- Cost Lab language that claims a switch is tax-efficient, suitable, or recommended for a specific investor.
- Investor Readiness Gate language that says an investor is ready to buy, sell, switch, redeem, or execute a transaction.
- Alert language that implies a trigger is a personalized transaction recommendation.
- Decision pack language that says a transaction should be made for a specific investor.
- Portfolio intelligence language that says an investor should buy, sell, switch, hold, redeem, or allocate a specific percentage.
- Portfolio Blueprint Lab language that says a weight is optimal, suitable, approved, recommended, or personally correct for an investor.
- Rebalance Guard language that says drift requires a buy, sell, switch, hold, redemption, pause, or exact allocation action.
- Portfolio Review Room language that says the user is cleared, approved, certified, or instructed to execute because the review score is high.
- Review Vault language that treats an improving score as authorization to invest, switch, redeem, or skip evidence review.
- Investor Record Desk language that treats a saved record as advice, suitability approval, account verification, distributor authorization, or a transaction instruction.
- Research Dossier Builder language that treats an assembled packet as a recommendation, model portfolio, suitability certificate, execution instruction, or verified account file.
- Fund House Lens language that says a fund house is approved, rejected, recommended, or unsuitable for a specific investor.
- Doc Decoder language that treats a document clause, riskometer, cost field, or return table as a personalized recommendation or final suitability conclusion.
- Review Rhythm language that says a review date is a buy, sell, switch, pause, hold, redeem, or transaction date.
- Live-data claims without source date, citation path, and freshness status.
- Return guarantees or phrases that imply assured outcomes.
- Execution of mutual fund transactions.
- Distributor client management without ARN/EUIN/PAN compliance design.
- Paid-plan language that implies subscribed users receive personalized advice.

## Product language

Use: research, route, category lane, local profile, profile discipline, research-compatible, conditional research, caution profile, change review, research pressure, decision lanes, candidate research set, fresh-money checkpoint, contribution-first route, drift review, tolerance band, rebalance guard, portfolio review room, review vault, review snapshot, investor review record, research dossier, evidence audit packet, record code, decision boundary, review queue, review lane, review file, review rhythm, review checkpoint, 30/60/90 review, goal lens, fit map, red flag, risk prompt, peer benchmark, peer lens, stewardship lens, house review, doc decoder, plain-English clause, must-read clause, blueprint draft, research weights, sleeve mix, process clarity, capacity comfort, manager visibility, style stability, shortlist, compare note, stress scenario, bad market rehearsal, market panic triage, wait window, allowed research action, locked action, no-panic rule, cost scenario, readiness gate, behavior prompt, watchlist, review trigger, decision support, decision memo, education, evidence, portfolio x-ray, role map, duplication risk, source date, citation status.

Avoid: advisory, recommendation, guaranteed, best fund for you, assured return.

## Monetization boundary

The Phase 1 paid tier may charge for software convenience, research history, watchlists, alerting, decision memo history, exports, and workflow organization. It should not charge for personalized recommendations unless the regulatory posture changes. Pricing copy should remain transparent and should not suggest that NiveshNadi is compensated by AMCs or distributors in Phase 1.

## Phase 2 requirement

Before building distributor workflows, define whether the product is:

- A neutral research software vendor.
- A Mutual Fund Distributor workflow product.
- A SEBI Investment Adviser workflow product.
- A hybrid product with strict separation between distribution and advisory.
