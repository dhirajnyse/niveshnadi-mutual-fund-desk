# Nadi Decision Pack

Nadi Decision Pack is the Phase 1 bridge between research and investor discipline. It turns the currently selected fund, Goal Fit Compass inputs, X-Ray comparison set, watchlist triggers, and the investor's own stated reason into one explainable memo.

Phase 1 v155 adds the Decision Flight Recorder. The pack now creates a black-box record for each memo: what thesis is being preserved, what evidence could prove the investor wrong, which future-self scenario creates pressure, which route must be opened before behavior changes, and which receipt fields would be needed later for account history or reviewer handoff.

Phase 1 v156 adds Nadi Anti-Hype Court. The pack now cross-examines the memo before confidence is allowed to survive. Evidence, behavior, cost, portfolio role, peer alternatives, and reason quality each become an objection with a proof route, demand, and no-confidence order.

Phase 1 v157 adds Nadi Regret Lab. The pack now interviews the investor's future self before the decision is trusted. Panic after a red statement, boring SIP months, peer envy, cost regret, emergency cash shock, and stale review drift become survival scenes with witness language, clauses, routes, and receipt fields.

Phase 1 v158 adds Nadi Motive MRI. The pack now scans the hidden motive behind the memo so return chasing, borrowed conviction, tax rush, brand comfort, cheapness shortcuts, and action bias cannot disguise themselves as research.

Phase 1 v159 adds Nadi Decision Multiverse. The pack now runs four competing research futures before confidence is trusted: act after all locks, watch first, low-cost peer challenge, and protect cash first. The strongest timeline becomes a route to inspect, not advice to execute.

## Product Purpose

Retail investors often jump from a fund card to an action without writing down why the fund fits the job. The Decision Pack slows the workflow down. It asks for the intended action, SIP amount, conviction level, next review date, and reason. It then creates a structured pack with:

- Selected fund and intended decision.
- Category, risk band, score, expense, return demo, and drawdown.
- Goal Fit context from the current compass settings.
- X-Ray comparison names already selected by the user.
- Watchlist triggers connected to the fund.
- Evidence checklist and guardrails.
- Decision Flight Recorder with black-box score, thesis, counter-thesis, future-self promise, and replay receipt fields.
- Anti-Hype Court with cross-examination, jury objections, no-confidence order, and defense packet fields.
- Regret Lab with future stress scenes, future-self oath, escape clauses, and regret receipt fields.
- Motive MRI with hidden-motive slices, detox routes, forbidden motive language, and MRI receipt fields.
- Decision Multiverse with four research timelines, a best path, a timeline to slow down, receipt fields, and rules for keeping the output research-only.
- Copy-ready text and a Journal save action.

## Compliance Posture

The pack is a self-research memo, not personalized advice. It should describe what the user selected, what evidence is visible, and what should be reviewed next. It must not say that the user should buy, sell, switch, hold, or redeem a fund. The pack should continue to use terms such as research, decision support, review, watchlist, evidence, and memo.

The Flight Recorder uses the same posture. It can say a memo is replayable or blocked, but it must not approve an investment action. Its strongest wording is procedural: preserve the original reason, review counter-evidence, open the weakest route, and treat stale or missing evidence as a blocker.

Anti-Hype Court is also procedural. It challenges the memo; it does not approve a fund. Its role is to force source proof, cost defense, peer comparison, behavior rehearsal, and written reasoning before the investor treats confidence as durable.

Regret Lab stays procedural as well. It does not predict returns or tell the user to invest. It rehearses moments where investors commonly break discipline and creates clauses for what to open before reacting.

Motive MRI is also procedural. It scans the stated reason and current context for motive contamination; it does not infer intent as fact or approve action. Its job is to force the user to rewrite a cleaner research motive before preserving the memo.

Decision Multiverse is procedural too. It compares possible research futures, but it must not label any future as a buy, sell, switch, hold, or redeem instruction. Its strongest wording is route-based: open evidence, watchlist, cost lab, or profile room before changing behavior.
