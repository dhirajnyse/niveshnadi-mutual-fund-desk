# Nadi Retail Glossary

Nadi Retail Glossary is the plain-language layer for retail investors who are blocked by mutual fund jargon. The goal is not to turn the product into a tutorial page; it is to translate confusing words into practical research checks inside the same decision workflow.

The glossary explains terms such as SIP, STP, NAV, TER, drawdown, riskometer, benchmark, exit load, direct versus regular plan, AUM, IDCW, and tracking error. Each term has four parts:

- plain-English meaning,
- why it matters,
- the research check an investor should perform,
- and a selected-fund translation using the current demo fund context.

The feature is intentionally research-only. It does not say whether the investor should buy, sell, switch, redeem, or choose one plan over another. It helps the investor understand what question to ask next, then routes that question to tools such as Cost Reality Lab, Risk Stress Lab, Evidence Ledger, Doc Decoder, or Decision Pack.

This layer is useful for Phase 1 because retail investors often know the word "SIP" but not the surrounding checks: horizon, drawdown, expense drag, category role, duplicate exposure, evidence freshness, and written decision discipline. It also prepares the product for Phase 2 distributor workflows because a shared glossary can reduce misunderstanding between investors and distributors.

Launch guardrails:

- Do not use glossary definitions as personalized investment advice.
- Do not collect PAN, folio, CAS, bank data, account credentials, or personal financial records in the glossary.
- Before live use, connect source citations for regulatory terms, KIM/SID language, riskometer labels, TER plan-class differences, tax-sensitive concepts, and exit-load clauses.
- Keep tax and execution-sensitive terms framed as research prompts unless reviewed by qualified compliance and regulatory specialists.
